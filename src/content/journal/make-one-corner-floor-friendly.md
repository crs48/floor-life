Floor living can sound like a renovation with a dress code: empty the room, buy linen, become extremely flexible. No wonder a perfectly good sofa starts looking nervous.

Here is a smaller experiment: one corner, one comfortable seat, and a stable way back up.

Try it before making the floor responsible for your entire personality.

## Choose one corner

Not a room. A corner. Somewhere you already like to be — beside a window, next to the bookshelf, wherever the afternoon light lands. You're going to make that one spot the single most inviting place in the room to be low to the ground.

Here's the whole recipe:

- **A secure rug** over a non-slip pad, with edges that cannot trip you.
- **A firm cushion or folded blanket** to try different seat heights. You may already own what you need.
- **A low surface** for your book or drink, plus a separate stable support for getting up. A light coffee table may not bear your weight.
- **A plant** and **warm light**. This is the part people skip, and it's the part that makes you want to stay.

That's it. You haven't thrown anything away. You haven't committed to anything. You've just built a small, beautiful trap.

## Let the corner do the work

Make the corner easy to use: put a book there, keep the cushion handy, and give yourself room to change position. A pleasant setup can lower the effort of trying something new. It cannot guarantee a habit, and that is fine.

> Don't try to sit on the floor more. Make one place where sitting on the floor is the obvious thing to do.

Give it a few short visits. If you enjoy it, keep it. If the corner needs more support, a different seat height, or a better light, adjust it. If floor sitting does not suit you, the same inviting spot can hold a chair.

When you invite someone over, offer both raised and low seating. The best room lets guests choose without having to explain their knees.

Floor living isn't a vow. It's a corner that keeps getting bigger.
